# Air hockey shared-table pilot

Scope: the three existing air hockey tables only. Keep this change in draft until the signed-in staging checks below are complete. No deployment or paid load test was performed for this draft. Other Arcade corrections and Cloud Hopper placement changes are separate work.

## Player flow and authority

A player can open the same table from either end. In a signed-in match, the first player keeps the end they approached and the shared table orientation follows that seat. A second player opening that table before it starts takes the opposite seat, resets readiness, and both must press **Face off**. The first player can press **Face off** alone to start with the existing AI mallet. There is no roster-generated opponent Ooga. Signed-in solo play has a presentation-only Clanker (the existing Arcade staff rig at the same 0.72 scale): it walks behind the far rail during the countdown, then follows the AI mallet. Its approach uses the server match start time, so late spectators see the current pose without extra movement packets. It has no account, voice peer, interactive target or simulation authority. Joining an active or finished session cannot replace either seat; the visitor returns to the hall to watch. Results reserve the table until a player returns to the hall, disconnects, or expires. Play again returns to the hall for a fresh reservation.

The first authenticated seat simulates fixed-step hockey physics. In two-human matches, both players move their mallet freely within their own half using the mouse or a finger drag; there is no smash button or power meter. The guest sends up to six ordered, bounded x/depth points per packet at 20 Hz. Exactly collinear points are coalesced; a six-point source overflow, a missing packet or a 500 ms idle gap causes collision-free resynchronization. The host queues at most 32 points and sweeps at most one per physics step; guest strike speed estimates divide the 50 ms packet interval equally among its retained points rather than treating each replay step as the sampling time; queue overflow similarly resynchronizes. These bounds can discard strokes during overload, but do not manufacture strikes across missing paths. The host also retains up to 32 ordered pointer points between physics updates, coalescing collinear motion; overflow resynchronizes without an invented strike. Both human mallets follow their clamped targets without a movement speed limit. The host sweeps each movement through the puck and caps effective mallet strike velocity at 6 m/s and puck speed at 7.5 m/s; a contact cannot apply repeated impulses while the mallet and puck overlap. The guest displays its own target immediately, while the host alone decides collisions. A stale guest target freezes its mallet after 500 ms. Solo retains the existing keyboard/touch slide, smash timing and AI. Both modes use a stable bird's-eye view, rotated so each human's own goal is at the bottom.

Every goal is exactly one point, with no bank, fire or win bonus. First to seven wins. All shared games, including signed-in solo, have no match clock; local practice retains the three-minute clock. Leaving before seven ends the match for both players, with no forfeit score or payout. After a completed match, the remaining player's result card stays visible when the other leaves. Shared play continues while its menu is open. Human matches still award no tickets; solo uses the existing ticket formula against the new goal score (one ticket at five or more goals). Stars use thresholds 1, 4 and 7.

Air hockey has no local or shared highscore: the HUD/result best rows, recording, submissions, subscriptions and public/API board are removed. Old local hockey values are ignored without losing unrelated saved progress. Existing D1 records remain stored but both GET and POST to `/api/highscores/hockey` return 404, and Arcade snapshots exclude hockey. The aggregate edge-cache key is versioned to avoid serving the retired board. Other games retain their score rules. The host remains trusted for physics and can manufacture bounded trajectories; this is not competitive anti-cheat.

`hockey-wire.js` is the browser/Worker contract; `hockey-session.js` owns the scene's subscription and seat lifecycle; `worker/src/hockey.js` owns three bounded sessions and `worker/src/hockey-limits.js` owns bounded admission/replay debt. The existing authenticated room WebSocket carries everything. The Worker advertises `hockey-v4`; an older Worker or signed-out page offers local practice. Every hockey command is versioned: older clients cannot join or drive a v4 match, while owned leave and cancellation remain available to prior clients. Restored older sessions are closed rather than mixed with v4; the admission-proof storage version remains unchanged. A signed-in page still connecting waits for its room before starting.

The Worker checks socket ownership through the existing room dispatcher, an owned non-gorilla body in `arcade.hall`, and proximity within 3 m of the table at floor height. It assigns seats from the authenticated player ID, ignores client identity, validates all 18 integer snapshot fields and limits the whole command to the existing 256-character JSON limit (the contract's emitted messages are ASCII). Only seat zero publishes snapshots; only seat one publishes inputs. Session IDs reject previous matches. Strictly increasing snapshot/input sequences reject duplicate and stale updates. Goals cannot decrease or advance by more than one per accepted frame, including the first frame compared with zero goals. The first frame cannot be terminal; shared frames must carry the untimed sentinel -1 and points equal to goals. A terminal frame requires a player at seven goals, and a seven-goal frame must be terminal. A dishonest host can still manufacture bounded gameplay or leave to end a match; this pilot does not claim server-authoritative physics.

Per-account token buckets allow 10 snapshots/s, 20 inputs/s and 2 control messages/s, each with a one-second burst. Releasing an owned seat bypasses an exhausted control burst so a quick join/start/exit does not strand a reservation. Only exact `arcade.hall` recipients receive gameplay; out-of-zone clients receive none. Late hall arrivals can receive the latest cached table state subject to the replay limits below. Guests interpolate the puck and remote mallet for 100 ms, then hold the last sample instead of extrapolating; their own mallet follows their local pointer. Spectators interpolate both mallets and the puck. A host neutralizes guest steering after 500 ms without input. The final frame field is a wrapping 16-bit collision counter; followers coalesce collision cues to one per accepted snapshot and derive goal/fire/end feedback from state transitions. The initial snapshot does not replay past sounds. Two-human play has no timing ring or smash cue; collision and goal sounds follow snapshots.

Unacknowledged joins retry once a second for at most ten seconds. Leaving before the reply sends an owned-lobby-only cancellation, which cannot terminate an active match. An unfinished session closure or a connection loss clears pause/results overlays; a terminal frame during a guest countdown reaches results. Each player heartbeats every two seconds. Either seat leaving, losing its body, changing zone, being replaced by another socket or disconnecting closes the session. A seat silent for 15 seconds, a lobby older than four minutes, or results older than four minutes, is removed on the next hockey message, hall arrival or existing room alarm. Thus a completely quiet room can display a stale table until the next existing alarm (normally at most one minute). There is no host migration or silent replacement of a disconnected human by AI. Reconnection requires joining again. Readiness already set for a seat is a no-op for table broadcasts.

Sessions, cached snapshots and active-connection packet buckets live in bounded WebSocket attachments and restore after hibernation. A new socket still resets its packet buckets; it cannot resume the old match and must pass durable admission/replay limits. No claim is made that exact frame/input debt survives socket replacement.

The local hardening adds one Durable Object KV entry, `hockeyLimitsV1`, loaded inside the existing constructor input gate. It holds room admission/replay buckets, three creation cooldowns, three admission proofs and at most 64 account entries. Each entry expires logically 60 seconds after its last accepted grant; expired entries are pruned on later use and saved with the next accepted grant. A full ledger refuses new accounts rather than evicting retained debt. No database migration, timer or service is added.

| Operation | Persistent bound |
| --- | --- |
| Any new seat, first or second | Room token bucket: 2 admissions/s, burst 2; each account waits 2 seconds between admissions |
| Creation of a table session | Each physical table waits 5 seconds between creations, even after immediate cancellation |
| Snapshot replay, including zone entry, explicit watch and an existing seat's repeated join acknowledgement | Room: 8 table payloads/s, burst 8; account: 1 payload/s, burst 3. A hall-entry batch pays for every table payload |
| Leaving, cancellation of an owned pending lobby, disconnect/zone/body cleanup | Immediate; never waits for a quota or storage operation |

Admission/replay grants update the ledger synchronously and call `storage.put` before replying. This avoids an asynchronous join/cancel queue. Cloudflare's default [output gates](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/glossary/) hold outgoing messages until outstanding storage writes succeed and reset the object on a failed write; `allowUnconfirmed` is never used. Local rejection also closes further grants. Because socket-attachment rollback is not assumed atomic with storage, each admission writes a fixed-slot proof containing the session ID, both authenticated account IDs and connection tokens alongside its debt. Restore runs only after loading this ledger and rejects the whole attached session unless every seat matches a committed version-2 proof. The three fixed-slot proofs have no wall-clock expiry, allowing untimed matches to survive hibernation; they are overwritten on the next admission and remain bound to both live connection tokens. They are independent of account-debt expiry. Cleanup need not rewrite them because a proof without a matching socket attachment cannot restore anything. The output-gate guarantee is documented, not reproduced by the fake-socket tests. Accepted grants cause at most `2 + 8 = 10` put calls/s plus a combined initial burst of 10 (before platform coalescing); frames, inputs, readiness and cleanup make no ledger writes. Existing room storage work is additional.

Hall watchers retry unconfirmed tables with the scene clock at most once per 1.1 seconds for 12 connected seconds, reset the window after a lost connection, then stop any stale cached table that was never confirmed. The listener remains active for future table broadcasts. Denied replay may therefore temporarily show no live table under contention. Reading a hall snapshot does not require driving a body; joining a match still does. Active messages still invoke the Durable Object and serialize attachments; this is not free computation.

## Geometry, avatars and sound

The playable and hall tables use the same 1.35 horizontal scale: the playing surface is now 1.485 m across and 2.97 m along, with its original 0.82 m height. Table centres stay at `(16.3, -4.75)`, `(-13.3, -14.15)` and `(13.55, -14.15)` in hall x/z. Seats move to centre z plus/minus 2.035 m; collision footprints expand with the rails. The original 1.1-by-2.2 simulation coordinates and packet bounds remain unchanged: rendering and pointer ray intersections apply the same scale in opposite directions. Puck, mallets, marks and goal effects use that mapping. Both views use one table model.

The hall's existing remote-player renderer shows real seated humans through ordinary room poses. Signed-in solo has one human and a Clanker on the AI side. Spectators see scores over head silhouettes; players see the smaller scores centered inside each goal half-circle, behind the resting mallet. Fixed hockey scoreboards and their collision blocks are removed, and the shared HUD does not duplicate the scores in cards. A tight orthographic overhead camera fills most of the height with the table, cropping the heads at the viewport ends; the player's own goal stays at the bottom. Both scene exits dispose the added rigs. Only hockey's fake opponent and nearby fake hockey watchers are removed here; broader crowd changes belong to the separate Arcade correction.

Hockey retains `arcade.hall`, the driven body and its physical seat pose through the lobby, countdown, game, menu and results. A narrowly scoped synchronous Arcade/hockey scene handoff suppresses the temporary body release that would otherwise clear the voice peer list. Other scene transitions keep the existing release contract. Failure during handoff releases the body. The page's existing voice controller remains the sole voice subscription owner; range, zone eligibility, individual mute, microphone mute and receive volume remain intact. Hall incidental sound sources are shared by `arcade-ambience.js`, heard from the seat with the existing 16 m attenuation and stereo pan, through the existing environment-mute output. No new audio context or voice subscription is added.

## Traffic, latency and cost estimates

These formulas describe the configured rates, not measured production usage. A two-human table generates about `10 frames + 20 inputs + 1 heartbeat = 31` incoming messages/s, excluding existing poses/control/setup. One solo table generates `10 + 0.5 = 10.5`/s. At the existing 32-player room limit, one table can fan out 320 frame deliveries/s, plus 20 input relays/s; three tables can fan out 960 frame deliveries/s plus 60 input relays/s. The 138.7-byte measured mean is the client frame; the server timestamp adds about 20 bytes. Snapshots currently echo to their host too.

The earlier v1 calculation using maximum-width fields (collision counter, maximum sequence and a 13-digit timestamp) gave **184 bytes per server frame** and **97 bytes per input relay**. At three tables/32 recipients, steady admitted gameplay is at most **1,020 deliveries/s and 182,460 JSON bytes/s**. Full token buckets permit an immediate burst of that size, and an arbitrary one-second window can approach twice it: **2,040 deliveries and 364,920 bytes**. These are historical v2 estimates, not ceilings for v3: v3 input packets can include six points and a version field; they have not been remeasured. These exclude WebSocket/TLS framing, ordinary poses/voice and control messages; they are not network measurements.

Local hardening replaces the earlier unrestricted lobby-churn estimate with persistent seat and creation limits above. The earlier replay ceiling was 264 JSON bytes. The match-start timestamp for synchronized Clanker approach adds at most 22 bytes; using a conservative 286-byte ceiling, the replay path is modeled at **2,288 bytes/s plus a 2,288-byte burst**, independent of room size because replies are unicast. An arbitrary one-second window can approach twice that. Session metadata broadcasts are charged indirectly through admissions and table creation: at most two joins, two changed readiness states, one terminal table state and one close per two-human session (six metadata broadcasts, excluding explicitly budgeted replay). The three five-second creation cooldowns permit at most 0.6 new sessions/s in the long run; initial/in-flight sessions add bursts. Readiness spam does not add broadcasts, and repeated-seat acknowledgements use replay quota.

These are path-specific bounds, **not a global room output, request or CPU cap**. Existing zone broadcasts, connection/welcome/replacement work, small busy replies and parsing rejected requests remain outside them. Persisted guarantees cover admission and replay across reconnect/hibernation, not every raw packet across reconnect. The hardening's maximum modeled 36,000 ledger put calls/hour under sustained accepted-grant pressure are additional storage operations; they are not measured billable rows or an observed cost. Ordinary matches should need far fewer grants.

Guest steering has up to 50 ms input scheduling, up to 100 ms snapshot scheduling and 100 ms interpolation, plus physics scheduling and network legs guest→room→host→room→guest. The rough scheduling contribution is 175 ms on average and up to 250 ms before network/physics; this is an estimate, not measured latency. These estimates describe the former snapshot-driven guest presentation and target smoothing; the current local mallet presentation bypasses both. Host-authoritative puck responses still depend on the network and should be measured before expansion.

Cloudflare's [Durable Objects pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/) and [WebSocket hibernation guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/) were checked on 2026-11-03. Paid request pricing is $0.15/million beyond the included 1 million, with incoming WebSocket messages converted at 20:1; outgoing messages do not incur that request charge. At steady rates this is approximately $0.000837 request cost per two-human table-hour, or $0.000284 per solo table-hour, **after included allowances**, before existing room traffic, additional admission/replay requests, ledger storage writes and other services. All three two-human tables are approximately $0.002511/hour of marginal request cost under those assumptions.

Duration remains separate: the documented 128 MB allocation means a fully awake room-hour is approximately 450 GB-s, or $0.005625 beyond the included 400,000 GB-s at $12.50/million GB-s. That is a shared-room bound, not a measured incremental game cost or a per-table charge. Hibernation may lower billed duration between events, but active traffic may keep the room awake. Worker/DO CPU, actual hibernation, billing, real transport latency and SFU usage were not available locally. No paid benchmark or deployment was performed.

Do not extrapolate the local physics-loop time to DO CPU.
